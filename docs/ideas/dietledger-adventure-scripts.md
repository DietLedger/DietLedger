# DietLedger: The next chapter starts at your table

**Draft scripts — sketch storyboards available; not implemented.**

**Visual review:** [Sketch storyboard gallery](../storyboards/index.html) · [All paths contact sheet](../storyboards/contact-sheet.png).

**Medium clarification:** All narrative/scene animations are intended to run as native HTML, inline SVG, CSS and small JavaScript controllers—not rendered video or Remotion playback. The optional genuine product-demonstration capture plan below is separate from the narrative animation system; this storyboard task records no media and does not finalize the later demo format.

## Creative brief

Three opening doors, four complete journeys, one destination: beginning a self-hosted DietLedger setup.

- **For myself:** a day in your future life. Intimate, grounded, quietly empowering.
- **For my family:** an optimistic journey into tomorrow. Warm, expansive, hopeful.
- **For people in my care:** a shared invitation followed by two branches: school meals and care for older people or people living with illness. Generous, practical, dignified.

The distinction is the setting, not someone's age: care within your own household belongs in Family; school, professional, and community care belongs in the care branches.

Existing DietLedger identity stays intact. Cinematic pacing comes from composition and transitions, not forced waiting. Visitors control progression. Back, Change path, and Set up DietLedger remain available throughout. Do not collect diagnoses or personal health details to personalize this public site.

**Script convention:** headlines and body copy are visitor-facing text. They may also support optional future narration, but no audio or autoplay narration is required. Motion and demo notes are production directions, not claims about existing media. All illustrations must be original or appropriately licensed. No fabricated testimonials, results, or endorsements.

## Journey 1 — For myself
### A day with more intention

**Mood:** Morning light to evening warmth. Botanical green, cream, restrained copper. The visitor is the protagonist; no idealized body imagery or before-and-after transformation.

### Scene P1 — Morning: Begin with yourself

**Headline**
> Your needs deserve a place in the plan.

**Body**
> Maybe you're eating around a particular condition. Maybe you're paying closer attention to nutrition. Maybe you simply want your meals to reflect what matters to you.
>
> Start there. Not with someone else's perfect day—with your own next step.

**Next:** See what could fit

**Motion:** Morning light moves across a recipe card. Scattered notes settle beside it; the composition becomes calmer without implying an automated diagnosis or personalized prescription.

### Scene P2 — Discovery: See the possibilities

**Headline**
> Find possibilities. Understand the details.

**Body**
> Explore recipes against nutrition targets you can review and adjust. Open the nutrition behind a recipe, see what's reported, and notice what isn't.
>
> When you have specific dietary needs, let that information support the eating plan you choose with your care team.

**Demo:** Show a fictional user's editable Diabetes-aware profile, the opt-in recipe filter, and one recipe's nutrition. Keep the real interface labels, including the current Diabetic filter button. Do not animate every recipe into a successful match.

**Visible demo caption**
> An editable recipe-discovery screen—not a medical recommendation. Missing required nutrition does not count as a confirmed match.

**Next:** Make it part of the day

**Motion:** Candidate recipe cards arrange around the selected recipe. The selected card moves forward; the others remain possibilities, not forbidden foods.

### Scene P3 — Preparation: Make tomorrow easier

**Headline**
> Give tomorrow one less thing to figure out.

**Body**
> Put a recipe on your meal plan. Choose the servings you intend to prepare. Turn the plan into a shopping list that brings the ingredients together.
>
> An intention becomes something you can actually bring home and cook.

**Demo:** Add a recipe to a meal plan, show planned servings, then open the generated grocery list. Show actual supported actions rather than simulating an automatic planner.

**Next:** See the bigger picture

**Motion:** The recipe card settles into a calendar. Ingredient names travel along the same copper thread into a shopping list.

### Scene P4 — Evening: Return to your own story

**Headline**
> One day is a beginning. Keep the story going.

**Body**
> Log what you eat and review nutrition over time. Return to your recipes, revisit your goals, and make the next plan with more context than you had before.
>
> Your life is bigger than a number. Give yourself a workspace that keeps the details together.

**Demo:** A food-log entry and a nutrition overview using clearly labeled fictional data. No invented health improvement, glucose prediction, or weight-loss result.

**Primary CTA:** Build my nutrition workspace

**Transition to setup:** The day's thread curves around the DietLedger logo and becomes a small workspace window.

## Journey 2 — For my family
### The tomorrow we build together

**Mood:** Expanding horizons, warm kitchens, shared tables. Start small and gradually reveal a larger picture. Avoid portraying a single household structure as the default.

### Scene F1 — Tonight: Start where life happens

**Headline**
> The future doesn't always start with a grand plan.

**Body**
> Sometimes it starts with a familiar question: what's for dinner?
>
> A recipe someone loves. A little thought about what goes into it. A meal that fits the day you actually had.
>
> That is somewhere worth beginning.

**Next:** Give the week some shape

**Motion:** A single recipe becomes a place setting. Light spreads across an illustrated table; people need not be depicted or assigned idealized roles.

### Scene F2 — The week: Make space for real life

**Headline**
> A little planning can make room for a lot of living.

**Body**
> Bring your recipes into a meal plan. Look at the days ahead, choose what you want to cook, and adjust the servings you plan to make.
>
> Not a perfect week. A week with somewhere to start.

**Demo:** Manually build a short meal plan from saved recipes. Show the actual application, not a fictional family-member nutrition dashboard.

**Next:** Bring the plan home

**Motion:** Place settings resolve into calendar spaces. The camera widens to reveal several days without locking the visitor into a timed sequence.

### Scene F3 — Shopping: Connect the little decisions

**Headline**
> Let the meals shape the shopping list.

**Body**
> Turn your meal plan into a grocery list. Planned servings shape ingredient quantities, and ingredients come together in one place.
>
> Take the next step with a list connected to what you intend to cook—not another set of notes to assemble from scratch.

**Demo:** Generate a list from the preceding plan. Show an actual ingredient aggregation; do not imply budgeting, retailer checkout, stock checking, or procurement features.

**Next:** Keep what matters

**Motion:** Calendar cards send ingredient lines toward a paper-like list. The list rests beside a table rather than becoming a delivery or ordering interface.

### Scene F4 — Tomorrow: Keep something worth passing on

**Headline**
> Some recipes become part of the family story.

**Body**
> Keep the dishes you return to. Organize them into books. Create a printable cookbook for the recipes you want close at hand—or want to pass along.
>
> A plan for this week. Something to build on for the years ahead.

**Demo:** A recipe collection, book chapters, and a genuine exported cookbook page. Use original or public-domain recipe content with required attribution.

**Primary CTA:** Create our family's workspace

**Transition to setup:** The cookbook closes gently. Its cover becomes the frame of the visitor's own DietLedger workspace.

### Capture rules
- Use an isolated demo instance and fictional accounts. No real personal, patient, student, credential, or host information.
- Use recipes and artwork with permission or a clear public-domain/license basis; retain attribution where required.
- Capture real behavior, including loading and missing-data states where relevant. Do not composite unsupported controls or fabricate results.
- Keep UI text legible at mobile widths. Prefer a focused crop over an entire desktop dashboard.
- Aim for one understandable action per short clip; determine final durations after recording rather than forcing a time promise.
- Prefer compressed MP4/WebM with poster frames and explicit play/pause controls. Provide textual equivalents and still-image fallbacks. GIFs are optional derivatives for documentation, not the only way to understand the site.

## Interaction and motion rules
- Suggested transition length: roughly 400–700 ms, interruptible. Never make visitors wait for a scene to finish before navigating.
- Do not hijack scrolling or advance scenes automatically. Use visible progress such as Chapter 2 of 4.
- Preserve Back, Change path, and Set up DietLedger. Switching paths must not feel like failing a quiz.
- Support keyboard navigation, browser history/deep links, deliberate focus placement, and reduced motion. Detailed implementation belongs in the approved implementation spec.
- With JavaScript unavailable, the stories and installation destination must remain readable and reachable.
- No automatic audio, health-data collection, forced registration, countdowns, invented popularity, or guaranteed health outcomes.

## Claim review and remaining decisions

School and care stories express the user's desired audience and future, not a claim that DietLedger is operationally validated for those settings. There is no claim of regulatory meal compliance, HIPAA compliance, clinical certification, institutional procurement support, or successful school/care deployments.

Before implementation: approve this copy and scene structure; validate any further feature claims against current code; choose the first journey to prototype.
